# sonos-nest — the round knob controller

A standalone, wall-mountable Sonos knob. Twist for volume, press for play/pause, swipe to
browse. It talks **directly** to your Sonos speakers over the local network (UPnP/SOAP) — no
server, no cloud, no Home Assistant, no phone.

Build env: **`nest`**. OTA/mDNS hostname: **`sonos-nest`**.

---

## Hardware

**Board: ELECROW CrowPanel 2.1" HMI ESP32-S3 Rotary Display (DHE03921D).**

| | |
|---|---|
| MCU | ESP32-S3R8 — dual-core 240 MHz, **8 MB OPI PSRAM, 16 MB flash**, WiFi 2.4 GHz |
| Display | **ST7701**, 480×480 round IPS, 2.1", RGB-parallel |
| Touch | **CST816** capacitive, I²C `0x15` (*not* a GT911 — a common mis-spec) |
| Rotary encoder | EC11, GPIO **42/4**, hardware PCNT, 4 counts/detent |
| Knob press | A **separate tact switch (K112)** on a **PCF8574 expander @ 0x21, pin P5**, active-low |
| Backlight | GPIO 6, LEDC PWM (dimmable) |
| I²C bus | SDA 38 / SCL 39 @ 400 kHz (shared: touch + expander) |
| Power / programming | **4-pin MX1.25 JST on the back — NOT USB-C.** Ships with a 50 cm MX1.25→USB-A cable. 5 V / 1 A |

Two things that surprise people:

**The knob press is stiff.** It's a discrete tact switch under the knob, not the encoder's own
push. It wants a firm, *centered* push — an off-axis nudge often won't register.

**There is no software-controllable LED.** The board has a hard-wired power LED and nothing
else. `pins.h` defines a `PIN_ENCODER_LED` but nothing in the firmware drives it, and the board
HAL exposes no LED API. Don't plan a status light around it. (Haptics would need a DRV2605
add-on.)

### Physical / mounting

Full spec: [`hardware/round-nest-2.8/crowpanel-2.1-physical-spec.md`](../hardware/round-nest-2.8/crowpanel-2.1-physical-spec.md).

- Envelope **Ø79 × 30 mm**, ~80 g. Aluminium + plastic body, acrylic front.
- **The Ø79 front bezel rotates — it *is* the input.** Never clamp, grip, or overhang the
  front. **Mount from the rear only.** This is the single hard rule of any case for this unit.
- Rear body is **Ø58 mm**; a rear mount up to ~Ø61 mm hides behind the bezel.
- Rear fixing: **3 × M3 on a Ø12 mm bolt circle** in a raised centre hub (the circle is offset
  ~1 mm from the body axis — that's real, not measurement noise).
- The MX1.25 cable exits the **back**, so any mount needs a centre/rear pass-through (Ø11 clears
  the connector).

### Case: magnetic wall mount

[`hardware/round-nest-2.8/wall/`](../hardware/round-nest-2.8/wall/) — a two-piece
**magnetic pull-off wall mount**, the only case that exists for this unit today.

| Part | File | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Wall plate | `wall_plate.stl` | Ø68.8 × 7 mm. Screws to the wall (2 × #8/M4 countersunk, heads hidden inside the lip). Centre hole + edge notch route the cable. |
| Cradle | `cradle.stl` | Ø62 × 23.7 mm. Cups the Ø58 body, bolts to the rear hub with **3 short M3**. Carries magnets + an anti-rotation key tab. |

- **8 × Ø6×3 mm N52 magnets** (4 per part), glued into blind pockets. Mind polarity — all plate
  magnets one way, all cradle magnets the other. Dry-stack before the glue sets.
- Attach/detach is a **straight pull-off / push-on, no twisting** — deliberate, because the
  bezel spins freely and you can't grip the hidden body to twist a bayonet.
- Print in **PETG or ASA** (PLA can creep near warm electronics). 0.2 mm layers, 3+ perimeters,
  30–40% infill. Cradle **rim-down** (magnet pockets and key tab then print support-free);
  plate flat, lip up. No supports for either.
- Geometry is generated by Python CSG (`trimesh` + `manifold3d`), not OpenSCAD:
  `python3 build_all.py` regenerates the STLs; `gen_paper_templates.py` prints 1:1 drill
  templates.

> **Status:** the screw pattern, diameters, cable routing and a digital fit-check all pass, but
> the mount has **not yet been printed and hung**. Magnet pull and bezel clearance are unproven
> on real parts. Print the cradle first as a first-article.

## What it does

Everything below is live in firmware today.

**Now Playing** (the home screen) — album art, title, artist, room name, a 270° progress arc,
and elapsed/total time with a play/pause/stop glyph.

- **Twist = volume.** Accelerated: spin fast and each detent counts for up to 6×, so you can
  cross the whole range in a flick but still land on a single step when you turn slowly.
- **Press = play/pause.** Responds instantly (optimistic) with a ripple animation, then
  reconciles with the speaker.
- **Tap ◀ / ▶** (big circular targets, left and right) = previous / next track.

**Gestures from Now Playing:**

| Gesture | Goes to |
|---|---|
| Swipe **up** | **Queue** — the live queue; tap a track to jump to it |
| Swipe **down** | **Clock** |
| Swipe **right** (drag in from the left edge) | **Menu** |

> The Menu is reached by **swiping in from the left edge**, *not* by long-pressing the knob.
> (A long press does nothing on Now Playing. Some older comments in the tree still say
> otherwise — they're stale.)

**Menu** — Now Playing · Rooms · Group · Playlists · Favorites · Settings. Twist to scroll,
press or tap to choose, swipe left to go back.

**Rooms** — every Sonos zone discovered on the network. Pick one and it becomes the room this
knob controls; the choice is saved and restored on the next boot.

**Group** — join and un-group rooms. Rooms sharing the current room's coordinator are ticked;
the active room is marked and can't toggle itself. Selecting another room joins or leaves it.

**Playlists / Favorites / Queue** — one browse list, three sources. Playlists clear the queue
and enqueue; Favorites set the transport URI directly; Queue seeks within what's already
loaded.

**Clock** — big NTP clock with the date. It also arrives on its own as a **screensaver** after
30 s idle *when nothing is playing*, dimmed to 12% backlight. Any input wakes it. Swiping down
from Now Playing pins it manually, and that one won't auto-dismiss.

**On every list screen:** twist = move, press = select, **long press = back**, drag = scroll.

**Settings** — brightness only (10–100%, 5% steps, saved to NVS), plus a footer showing the OTA
hostname and the device's **IP address**. That IP is what you need for wireless flashing.

**Not implemented** (so you don't go looking): TuneIn radio browsing, music-library browsing,
lyrics, on-device WiFi setup, MQTT/Home Assistant. WiFi credentials come from `secrets.h`.

## Setup

### 1. Credentials

```bash
cp include/secrets.example.h include/secrets.h
```

Fill in `WIFI_SSID` and `WIFI_PASS` — these are **required**; the nest has no on-device WiFi
setup screen. Optional but recommended:

| Define | Why |
|---|---|
| `SONOS_DEFAULT_ROOM` | The room to control before you've picked one on-device |
| `CLOCK_TZ` | POSIX timezone for the clock (defaults to US Pacific) |
| `OTA_PASSWORD` | Required for wireless flashing — set it now, on the first USB flash |
| `SONOS_ZONE_IP` | Dev-only: skip discovery and point straight at a speaker |

`include/secrets.h` is gitignored.

### 2. Build and flash over USB

```bash
export PATH="$PATH:$HOME/.platformio/penv/bin"
pio run -e nest
pio run -e nest -t upload --upload-port /dev/ttyACM0
```

The **first** USB flash needs download mode: hold **BOOT**, tap **RST**, release **BOOT**.
Later flashes reset themselves.

On WSL, the port is exposed with `usbipd` from an admin PowerShell, it drops on every device
reset, and the port number bumps — see [`flashing-wsl.md`](flashing-wsl.md) for the whole
story. Resolve the port dynamically rather than hardcoding it:

```bash
pio run -e nest -t upload --upload-port "$(ls /dev/ttyACM* | head -1)"
```

### 3. First boot

It connects to WiFi, syncs time, discovers Sonos, and picks a room (saved room → `SONOS_DEFAULT_ROOM`
→ first one found). If it lands on the wrong room, swipe in from the left edge → **Rooms**.

Zone IPs are cached in NVS, so later boots skip the ~3.6 s discovery wait.

## Managing it

### Wireless updates (OTA)

Once it's on WiFi with an `OTA_PASSWORD` flashed, you never need the cable again.

Get the device's IP from **Settings** on the device, then:

```bash
pio run -e nest-ota -t upload --upload-port <device-ip>
```

The device shows an update overlay with a progress bar and reboots itself when done. A failed
or interrupted transfer is harmless — the running firmware isn't touched until the upload
completes, and it auto-reboots after 20 s of no progress.

If you're working in this repo with Claude Code, the **`/ota` skill** does all of this for you
(firewall check, mDNS lookup, password) — it's the easiest path.

### Brightness

Menu → Settings, then twist. Saved automatically.

### Changing rooms

Menu → Rooms. Persisted across reboots.

### Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause |
|---|---|
| Knob press does nothing | It's a stiff separate switch — push firmly, dead centre. |
| Volume moves but nothing plays | You're on the right room but nothing's queued — try Playlists or Favorites. |
| Transport works, volume doesn't (or vice versa) | Expected in groups: **transport goes to the group coordinator, volume is per-speaker.** |
| Blank/negative colours after a display change | Wrong panel init — the ST7701 path is pinned to Arduino_GFX 1.3.1 on purpose. |
| Can't find it for OTA | Read the IP off the Settings screen; DHCP may have moved it. |
